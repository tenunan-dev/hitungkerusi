# Malaysian Political Parties — GE16 Landscape

**Compiled:** 4 August 2026 | **Status:** Living document — updated as party dynamics evolve

---

## Core Insight: Coalitions are convenience, not identity

The "Kerajaan Perpaduan" (Unity Government) is a **post-election arrangement**, not an electoral bloc. Voters do not vote for "the government." They vote for parties and coalitions. At the state level, PH and BN compete directly against each other. In Johor (July 2026) and Negeri Sembilan (August 2026), PH and BN contested all seats head-to-head.

The actual electoral landscape is **multi-polar**: PH, BN, PN, GPS, GRS, WARISAN, and emerging forces (Bersama, MUDA, KDM, PBM) each have independent strategies, voter bases, and coalition options.

## Part I — Major Coalitions

### Pakatan Harapan (PH)
**Seats:** 77 (DAP 40, PKR 29, AMANAH 8) | **PM:** Anwar Ibrahim (PKR)
**Character:** Multi-racial reformist coalition. PKR = Malay centrist urban/suburban base. DAP = Chinese/urban secular base. AMANAH = Malay progressive Islamist base.
**GE16 posture:** Incumbent but competing against BN in state elections. Anwar is PM but PH cannot take BN's support for granted — BN may go solo.

### Barisan Nasional (BN)
**Seats:** 30 (UMNO 26, MCA 2, MIC 1, PBRS 1) | **Leader:** Ahmad Zahid Hamidi (UMNO)
**Character:** Malay-establishment coalition. UMNO dominates. MCA and MIC are shells of their former selves.
**GE16 posture:** "Almost certain to go solo" (Zahid, Aug 2026). Won Johor (+17pp) and N9 (+14pp) — strengthened solo argument. Key question: does BN cooperate with PH federally while competing locally, or break entirely?

### Perikatan Nasional (PN)
**Seats:** 74 (PAS 43, WAWASAN 6, BERSATU rump 6, GERAKAN 0) | **Unofficial leader:** Abdul Hadi Awang (PAS)
**Character:** Malay-Islamist opposition. PAS is the dominant force (43/74 seats). Bersatu was reduced to a rump (6 MPs) after the June 2026 purge. WAWASAN (Hamzah faction, 19 ejected Bersatu MPs) was admitted to PN but has weak machinery.
**Critical dynamic:** PAS-Bersatu split (8 June 2026). PAS refused to work with Bersatu. WAWASAN replaced Bersatu in PN. Bersatu contests solo → splits Malay vote in its seats → benefits BN/PH. This is the single most consequential party event for GE16.

### Gabungan Parti Sarawak (GPS)
**Seats:** 23 (PBB 14, PRS 5, SUPP 2, PDP 2) | **Leader:** Abang Johari
**Character:** Sarawak-first regional coalition. Supports federal government for development funds but competes against PH locally. GPS is essentially a single-party state — PBB dominates.
**GE16 posture:** Stable. No realistic challenge from PH/PN in Sarawak. Redelineation could add up to 35% more seats.

### Gabungan Rakyat Sabah (GRS)
**Seats:** 7 (GAGASAN 2, PBS 1, STAR 1, others) | **Leader:** Hajiji Noor
**Character:** Sabah-first regional coalition but more fragmented than GPS. Multiple parties with overlapping bases.
**GE16 posture:** Volatile. Sabah 2025 state election surprised everyone — WARISAN won 25/73 DUNs, GRS won 22. The federal projection for Sabah seats carries high uncertainty.

## Part II — Individual Parties (seats held, character, GE16 posture)

### PAS (Parti Islam Se-Malaysia) — 43 seats (largest single party)
- **Ideology:** Political Islam, Malay supremacy, conservative
- **Leader:** Abdul Hadi Awang
- **Voter base:** Rural Malay Belt (Kelantan, Terengganu, Kedah, Perlis)
- **GE16 posture:** Dominant in PN. Controls PN's direction. Has signaled 'vote for BN' in seats it doesn't contest — strategic ambiguity toward BN-PAS pact. Can operate independently of any coalition due to strong grassroots machinery.

### DAP (Democratic Action Party) — 40 seats
- **Ideology:** Social democracy, multiculturalism, secular
- **Leader:** Anthony Loke Siew Fook
- **Voter base:** Urban Chinese, mixed urban (Penang, Selangor, KL, Perak towns)
- **GE16 posture:** PH's most reliable vote bank. Non-Malay seats are safe. Vulnerable to being portrayed as "anti-Malay" by PN. Loke resigned as N9 DAP chair (Aug 2026) — internal renewal.

### PKR (Parti Keadilan Rakyat) — 29 seats
- **Ideology:** Reformism, centrist, multi-racial
- **Leader:** Anwar Ibrahim
- **Voter base:** Urban/suburban Malay middle class, mixed seats
- **GE16 posture:** Anwar is PM but PKR's base is squeezed — Malay voters drift to PN, non-Malay voters to DAP. Bersama (Rafizi's splinter) directly targets PKR's urban Malay base. Most vulnerable PH component.

### UMNO (United Malays National Organisation) — 26 seats
- **Ideology:** Malay nationalism, conservatism, establishment
- **Leader:** Ahmad Zahid Hamidi
- **Voter base:** Rural/semi-urban Malay (Johor, N9, Pahang, Perak)
- **GE16 posture:** Resurgent after Johor (+17pp) and N9 (+14pp). Almost certain to contest GE16 solo (not under PH umbrella). The key swing party — if UMNO breaks from PH and contests solo, it changes the entire electoral map. If UMNO stays aligned, government holds.

### BERSATU (rump) — 6 seats
- **Ideology:** Malay nationalism, originally UMNO offshoot
- **Leader:** Muhyiddin Yassin
- **Voter base:** Eroded. Purged 19 MPs to WAWASAN. Now a rump.
- **GE16 posture:** Contests solo outside PN. Splits Malay vote in its seats → benefits BN. EVENT_SHOCKS in engine apply −6pp to Bersatu seats as anti-PN shock.

### WAWASAN (Parti Wawasan Negara) — 6 seats (effectively 19 MPs who defected)
- **Ideology:** Malay nationalist, PN-aligned
- **Leader:** Hamzah Zainudin
- **Voter base:** Bersatu defectors. Weak grassroots machinery.
- **GE16 posture:** Admitted to PN. PAS's preferred partner (weaker than Muhyiddin's Bersatu). Untested electorally. May not retain all 19 seats.

### AMANAH (Parti Amanah Negara) — 8 seats
- **Ideology:** Progressive Islam, reformist
- **Leader:** Mohamad Sabu
- **Voter base:** Urban Malay progressives (small niche)
- **GE16 posture:** PH component. Struggling to differentiate from PKR and PAS. Mostly safe seats but limited growth potential.

### GPS components: PBB (14), PRS (5), SUPP (2), PDP (2)
- **Character:** Sarawak-first, developmentalist, bumiputera dominance
- **GE16 posture:** Stable. GPS operates as a bloc. No internal fractures. 23 seats are safe.

### WARISAN — 3 seats
- **Ideology:** Sabah nationalism, multi-racial, progressive
- **Leader:** Shafie Apdal
- **Voter base:** Sabah coastal/mixed seats. Won 25/73 DUN seats in 2025 Sabah election (surprise outperformance).
- **GE16 posture:** Stronger than expected. Aligned with PH federally but operates independently in Sabah. Could gain seats if Sabah fragmentation continues.

### Bersama — 0 seats (new party)
- **Ideology:** Urban reform, anti-corruption, youth, gender equality
- **Leaders:** Rafizi Ramli, Nik Nazmi
- **Voter base:** Urban young professionals, PKR defectors
- **GE16 posture:** Targets 10-20 seats. 50% women candidates, 70% under-50. Johor evidence: siphons 3-6% from PH in urban seats. Spoiler for PH — every vote Bersama takes from PH helps PN or BN in marginal seats.

### Others: MUDA (1), KDM (1), PBM (1), IND (7)
- **MUDA:** Syed Saddiq's youth party. 1 seat (Muar). Struggling for relevance after Undi18 wave faded.
- **KDM:** Sabah-based. 2 seats.
- **PBM:** Zuraida's party. 1 seat. Marginal.
- **IND:** 7 seats (some are party-affiliated MPs who went independent). Key independents in battleground seats.

## Part III — Key Coalition Dynamics for GE16

1. **PH-BN cooperation is FEDERAL only.** At state level they compete. GE16 will likely see PH and BN as separate electoral entities — the "government" framing is misleading.

2. **PN is now PAS-dominant.** With Bersatu reduced to a rump and WAWASAN untested, PAS controls PN's strategy. PAS has signaled openness to BN cooperation — a BN-PAS pact would reshape the landscape.

3. **Bersama is the wildcard.** If it contests broadly (20+ seats), it hurts PH. If it limits to 5-10 target seats, impact is contained. The Pandan and Setiawangsa by-elections will be the first live test.

4. **East Malaysia operates on local logic.** GPS and GRS support whichever federal government can deliver development funds. Their alignment is transactional, not ideological.

5. **The 2026 state elections showed BN resurgence in the south** (Johor +17pp, N9 +14pp) and **continued PN dominance in the north** (Kedah, Kelantan, Terengganu unchanged). The electoral map is becoming regionally bifurcated: PN north, BN south, PH urban pockets, GPS/GRS east.
