## Part I — Party Ethnic Profiles

Each party has a distinct ethnic signature. This is not incidental — it IS the party's electoral identity.

### Malay-dominant parties (avg Malay > 70%)

| Party | Seats | Avg Malay% | Avg Chinese% | Avg Youth% | Base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PAS** | 43 | **85.0%** | 9.7% | 31.0% | Rural Malay Belt |
| WAWASAN | 6 | 79.5% | 12.6% | 31.3% | Bersatu defectors |
| BERSATU | 19 | 74.7% | 10.9% | 31.7% | Semi-urban Malay |

**Implication:** These three parties compete for the SAME voter base — Malay-majority semi-urban/rural seats. The PAS-Bersatu split is existentially destructive for both because their voters overlap. Bersatu's 19 seats averaging 74.7% Malay means almost all are in seats where PAS is also competitive. WAWASAN's 79.5% avg Malay means it directly cannibalizes PAS.

### Mixed-Malay parties (avg Malay 50-70%)

| Party | Seats | Avg Malay% | Avg Chinese% | Avg Youth% | Base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UMNO | 26 | **59.1%** | 15.4% | 29.1% | Semi-urban mixed Malay |
| AMANAH | 8 | 59.7% | 24.0% | 33.7% | Urban Malay progressive |
| PKR | 29 | **49.4%** | 32.6% | 29.7% | Urban/suburban mixed |

**Implication:** UMNO and PKR compete in overlapping territory (~50-60% Malay seats). AMANAH's profile is similar to UMNO's in Malay% but with higher Chinese% — suggesting AMANAH seats are more urban. PKR is the most multi-racial large party (49.4% Malay, 32.6% Chinese).

### Non-Malay parties (avg Malay < 30%)

| Party | Seats | Avg Malay% | Avg Chinese% | Base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DAP** | 40 | **24.9%** | 56.8% | Urban Chinese majority |

**Implication:** DAP's seats are ethnically distinct from every other party — 56.8% Chinese avg is unique. DAP does not compete with PAS, Bersatu, or UMNO for voters. It competes with PKR in mixed seats and with GPS components in Sarawak.

## Part IV — Party-Level Implications for GE16 Forecast

### 1. PAS (43 seats, avg 85% Malay)
PAS seats are the SAFEST in the country. At 85% avg Malay in rural Kelantan/Terengganu/Kedah, these seats will NOT flip regardless of national swings. PAS's challenge is not defense but expansion — can it break into UMNO's 55-65% Malay seats?

### 2. DAP (40 seats, avg 24.9% Malay)
DAP seats are also SAFE. Non-Malay majority seats are structurally locked for PH. DAP's risk is at the margins — Bersama siphoning urban progressive votes in seats like Pandan and Setiawangsa.

### 3. PKR (29 seats, avg 49.4% Malay) — MOST VULNERABLE
PKR sits at the ethnic median. Its seats are competitive from BOTH sides — PN can gain Malay votes, Bersama can siphon urban votes. Many projected PH→PN flips are PKR seats in mixed-Malay areas.

### 4. UMNO (26 seats, avg 59.1% Malay) — KEY SWING PARTY
UMNO's profile sits between PAS's Malay heartland and PKR's mixed urban base. BN's southern resurgence (Johor +17pp, N9 +14pp) happened in UMNO's core demographic. If UMNO goes solo in GE16, it contests against BOTH PH and PN — potentially gaining Malay seats from both.

### 5. BERSATU (19 seats, avg 74.7% Malay) — EXISTENTIAL CRISIS
Bersatu's seats overlap almost entirely with PAS's territory. After the split, Bersatu contests solo while PAS contests under PN. This splits the Malay vote in Bersatu-held seats → benefits BN (or PH in mixed areas). The −6pp event shock applied to Bersatu seats is justified.

### 6. Bersama (0 seats, targets mixed urban) — WILDCARD
Bersama's target demographic (urban reform Malay) overlaps most with PKR and AMANAH. The 3-6% siphon effect seen in Johor is concentrated in seats with high youth% and moderate Malay%. This directly hurts PH's margin in marginal mixed seats.
