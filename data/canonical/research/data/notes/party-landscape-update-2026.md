# Malaysia Party Landscape Update — PN, WAWASAN, BERSATU, Bersama, DAP, PKR

**Compiled:** 3 August 2026 | **Vintage of sources:** May–July 2026 | **Composition baseline:** 22 Jun 2026 (project) vs Wikipedia infobox (retrieved 3 Aug 2026)

---

## 1. Executive summary of the changes

Between the project's composition snapshot (22 June 2026) and this update, the Malaysian opposition has undergone its most consequential realignment since 2020:

1. **PAS broke with Bersatu on 8 June 2026** — formally ending the coalition that had defined Perikatan Nasional since its founding.
2. **WAWASAN (Parti Wawasan Negara)** was formed on 13 June 2026 by Hamzah Zainudin's ejected Bersatu faction (19 MPs) and was **admitted into PN the same day**; Hamzah was reinstated as Leader of the Opposition on 18 June 2026.
3. **Bersatu is reduced to a rump** — roughly 6 loyal MPs under Muhyiddin Yassin, its funds frozen, its role in PN effectively usurped by WAWASAN.
4. **Pejuang and Parti Cinta Malaysia (PCM) joined PN** in 2026, expanding the coalition to six components.
5. **Bersama (Parti Bersama Malaysia)** launched 17 May 2026 by Rafizi Ramli and Nik Nazmi (ex-PKR) — and its first electoral test in Johor (11 Jul 2026) confirmed it as a **spoiler for PH**, with all 15 candidates losing their deposits.
6. **DAP and PKR** remain inside PH but with visible strain: DAP's Anthony Loke has publicly ruled out quitting PH; PKR is bleeding — Rafizi's departure follows his 2025 loss to Nurul Izzah Anwar, and Rafizi predicts PKR "risks dying out after the Anwar era."

**Net effect on the GE16 projection:** the "PN surge" scenario of a unified 80–84-seat PN is now much less likely. The opposition is fragmented into four poles (PAS, WAWASAN, Bersatu rump, Bersama), each contesting overlapping Malay/urban territory. The most likely net beneficiary is the government — the same fragmentation dynamic that delivered BN a 48-seat supermajority in Johor.

## 2. Perikatan Nasional (PN): the coalition after the split

### 2.1 Current membership (six components)

| Component | Leader | Dewan Rakyat seats | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PAS** | Abdul Hadi Awang | 43 | The dominant partner; broke with Bersatu 8 Jun 2026 |
| **BERSATU** | Muhyiddin Yassin | ~19 (register) / ~6 (loyal) | Marginalised; funds frozen; survival question |
| **WAWASAN** (Parti Wawasan Negara) | Hamzah Zainudin | 6 | New (13 Jun 2026); takes Bersatu's place as PN's second force |
| **GERAKAN** | Dominic Lau | 0 | Multiracial window-dressing |
| **MIPP** | Punithan Paramsiven | 0 | Indian component |
| **PEJUANG** | Mukhriz Mahathir | 0 | Joined 2026; deputy chair of PN |

**PN leadership (post-split):** Chairman **Ahmad Samsuri Mokhtar** (Terengganu MB, PAS technocrat — installed May 2026 after Muhyiddin's PM-candidacy declaration angered PAS); Deputy Chairmen include Muhyiddin Yassin, Dominic Lau, Mukhriz Mahathir, Tuan Ibrahim Tuan Man, and **Hamzah Zainudin**; Secretary-General Takiyuddin Hassan; Elections Director Sanusi Md Nor.

### 2.2 The split, reconstructed

- **Feb 2026 — Bersatu's "night of long knives":** the party purged deputy president **Hamzah Zainudin** and 17–19 of his supporters (MPs and assemblymen), leaving a rump loyal to Muhyiddin.
- **8 Jun 2026 — PAS ends cooperation with Bersatu**, citing Bersatu's equivocal roles in attempts to topple MBs in Negeri Sembilan and Perlis, its blocking of new PN members, and its under-performance (Bersatu won 31 seats to PAS's 43 in GE15 despite contesting 83 seats to PAS's 62).
- **13 Jun 2026 — WAWASAN founded and admitted to PN the same day**; Hamzah restored as Opposition Leader (18 Jun).
- **22 Jun 2026 — Bersatu insists it will remain in PN**, but analysts (Fulcrum/ISEAS) describe it as consigned to "the skid row of Malaysian politics," possibly forced eventually to approach PH.

### 2.3 What it means

PAS has effectively replaced its ambitious, well-funded partner with a dependent one. WAWASAN brings Bersatu's ejected MPs but **no money and no machinery** — its leverage over PAS is minimal. PAS now has free rein to expand into Pahang, Perak and Selangor without negotiating seat allocations with a rival that out-performed it. For GE16, the PN *brand* stays, but the *bloc* is PAS-dominant with a weaker second force — and a rump Bersatu that may contest seats of its own.

## 3. WAWASAN (Parti Wawasan Negara / National Vision Party)

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | 13 June 2026 (took over Parti Cinta Malaysia, PCM, registered 9 Aug 2009); legalised as WAWASAN 6 Jul 2026 |
| **President** | Hamzah Zainudin (Larut MP; ex-Bersatu deputy president; sacked Feb 2026) |
| **Origin** | "Reset Malaysia" — 19 MPs/assemblymen ejected from Bersatu in the Feb 2026 purge |
| **Ideology** | Ketuanan Melayu + multiracial democracy + national conservatism + MA63/Sabah-Sarawak autonomy |
| **Affiliation** | Perikatan Nasional (since 13 Jun 2026) |
| **Slogan** | "Membina Konsensus Nasional Baharu" (Forming a New National Consensus) |
| **Seats** | Dewan Rakyat 6/222; DUN 8/611; Dewan Negara 1/70 |
| **Known MPs** | Hamzah Zainudin (Larut), Wan Ahmad Fayhsal (Machang), Saifuddin Abdullah (Indera Mahkota) + 3 others |

**Assessment:** WAWASAN's 6 federal seats make it the second-largest party in PN, but its strength is borrowed — the MPs came from Bersatu, the party shell from a defunct micro-party (PCM, founded by ex-Gerakan's Huan Cheng Guan), and its first state-elections test (Johor/N9, Jul–Aug 2026) was described by analysts as "victory built on borrowed strength." Its racial make-up is unusually mixed for a Malay-right party (a point FMT highlighted). Its long-term relevance depends on whether it can convert Bersatu's organisational remnants into a durable base before GE16; analysts predict a **"civil war over Malay seats"** as WAWASAN, PAS and the Bersatu rump all claim the same Malay-majority constituencies.

## 4. BERSATU (Parti Pribumi Bersatu Malaysia): from kingmaker to rump

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **President** | Muhyiddin Yassin (Pagoh MP) |
| **Seats** | ~19 in the parliamentary register; ~6 loyal to Muhyiddin after the Feb 2026 purge and June 2026 defections |
| **Funds** | Bank accounts frozen; largely bereft of resources in opposition |
| **Status** | Nominally still in PN (Bersatu's stated position, 22 Jun 2026); functionally superseded by WAWASAN |

**The decline, in numbers:**
- GE15 (Nov 2022): **31 seats** — Bersatu out-performed PAS despite contesting more seats
- Pre-Feb 2026: 6 MPs sacked for supporting Hamzah → **25**
- Feb 2026 purge: 17–19 sacked/suspended → **~6 loyalists** (incl. Muhyiddin)
- Jun 2026: the ejected MPs form WAWASAN, which takes Bersatu's place in PN's inner circle

**Assessment:** Bersatu's trajectory mirrors classic Malaysian party decline: a well-funded, ambitious partner becomes a threat to its senior coalition ally, gets purged internally, then out-manoeuvred externally. Its options are narrow: (a) reconcile with PAS on PAS's terms, (b) contest GE16 solo in its Malay heartland seats — splitting the anti-government vote and handing seats to PH/BN, or (c) the unthinkable per its own rhetoric: approach PH. Any of (b) or (c) materially changes the GE16 seat arithmetic in the north. The projection model's "PN" bloc should now be read as **PAS + WAWASAN**, with Bersatu as a wildcard, not a PN component.

## 5. Bersama (Parti Bersama Malaysia): the urban spoiler

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Launched** | 17 May 2026 (took over Malaysian United Party, registered 2016, a former MCA splinter) |
| **Leaders** | Rafizi Ramli (de facto; ex-PKR deputy president, ex-Economy Minister) & Nik Nazmi Nik Ahmad (co-leader; ex-PKR VP) |
| **President (formal)** | Syukri Razab |
| **Branding** | Blue & yellow; "kancil" (mousedeer) logo |
| **Seats vacated** | Pandan (Rafizi) & Setiawangsa (Nik Nazmi) — both resigned 18 May 2026, creating the two current vacancies |
| **Strategy** | Contest independently, no coalition alignment; target urban voters on cost-of-living and reform |
| **First test** | Johor state election, 11 Jul 2026 |

**The Johor result — the empirical proof of the spoiler effect:**
- Bersama fielded **15 candidates**; **all 15 lost their deposits** (Malaysian law: forfeit for <12.5% of votes)
- Average vote share: **3–6%** per seat
- RSIS analysis: Bersama "acted as a direct spoiler for PH, siphoning away progressive, urban protest votes that traditionally went to DAP or PKR"
- Net effect: **BN's 48-seat supermajority** (up from 40) — PH fell from 12 to 8 seats

**Assessment:** In Johor, a 3–6% siphon was the margin of defeat in several PH seats. Transferred to GE16's 36 federal battlegrounds — where PH holds ~9 seats by margins under 5% — a similar siphon could flip several. But Bersama's ceiling is low: analysts (Asia Group Advisers, Huayan Policy Institute) see its appeal limited to disgruntled PH voters in Selangor and KL, with no path to seats in the medium term. Its strategic choice — contest broadly (spoiler for PH, inadvertent ally of BN/PN) or narrowly (kingmaker) — is the same fork identified in the GE16 report, now empirically demonstrated in Johor.

**Complication:** Rafizi is under MACC investigation over the RM1.1bn Arm Holdings strategic collaboration approved during his tenure as Economy Minister — an investigation he calls politically motivated.

## 6. DAP: the anchor that will not leave

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Secretary-General** | Anthony Loke Siew Fook |
| **Seats** | 40 — the largest single party in PH and in the Dewan Rakyat |
| **Position** | Loke (17 Jul 2026, CNA): DAP will **not** leave PH before the term ends — "you can only play the role of opposition" if solo |

**Key signals:**
- **No exit:** Loke has publicly closed the door on DAP quitting PH, neutralising speculation that DAP would bolt after a poor showing in the 2026 state polls.
- **PH's engine:** with 40 seats, DAP supplies the largest block to PH (PKR ~29, Amanah ~8); PH's federal survival depends on DAP's urban strongholds.
- **State focus:** Loke personally oversaw the Johor campaign (PH won 8 seats) and PH targeted 20 seats in Negeri Sembilan; he acknowledges "setbacks, but not total rejection" — PH's vote share rose even where seats fell.
- **Coalition strain:** BN's decision to contest solo in Johor (25 of 36 contested seats in N9) tests the PH-BN alliance; Loke frames the tension as manageable — "different dynamics in every election."

**Assessment:** DAP is the stability anchor of the unity government. Its 40 seats are the least contestable block in Malaysian politics (Chinese-majority urban seats), and its leadership has chosen coalition loyalty over the populist appeal of going solo. The risk to DAP is not defection but **erosion** — Bersama siphoning its urban base, and Bersama's cost-of-living message landing with the very voters DAP needs to retain in the battlegrounds.

## 7. PKR: the party in question

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **President** | Anwar Ibrahim (PM) |
| **Deputy President** | Nurul Izzah Anwar (won 71.7% vs Rafizi's 28.3%, May 2025 party polls) |
| **Secretary-General** | Fuziah Salleh |
| **Seats** | ~29 federal (PH component) |
| **Losses** | Rafizi Ramli & Nik Nazmi resigned from Cabinet and the party (May 2026); 6 further MPs ("renegades") publicly supportive of Bersama but not yet joined |

**The 2025 leadership election as the turning point:** Rafizi's defeat by Nurul Izzah (the PM's daughter) in the deputy presidency removed him from the party's inner circle and set the stage for his exit. He then predicted, presciently for some, that **"PKR risks dying out after the Anwar era"** — citing party bureaucracy and failure to reinvent itself.

**Key signals:**
- **Punitive measures:** PKR is considering action against the six renegade MPs who attended Bersama's launch (Kesavan, Zahir Hassan, Baktiar Wan Chik et al.).
- **Full-term commitment:** a PKR leader publicly urged PH to serve its full term (Aug 2026) — signalling internal jitters about early GE16 timing.
- **Succession vacuum:** with Rafizi gone and Nurul Izzah elevated, PKR's post-Anwar question is now explicit and unresolved.

**Assessment:** PKR is the weakest of PH's three components structurally — mid-20s seats, an ageing leader, a succession question, and a breakaway bleeding its activist base. The Johor result (PH 12→8) was partly Bersama's doing; GE16 will test whether PKR can hold its 29 seats against both PN and Bersama. The two vacant seats (Pandan, Setiawangsa) — both formerly PKR — are the first contested ground.

## 8. Implications for the GE16 projection model

The party update requires four adjustments to the model and its interpretation:

| Model element | Before | After update |
|---|---|---|
| **PN bloc composition** | PAS + Bersatu + WAWASAN (unified) | **PAS + WAWASAN** unified; **Bersatu rump** a wildcard that may split the Malay vote |
| **PN surge scenario** | +5pp unified PN → 84 seats | Less likely: fragmentation caps PN closer to base case; a Bersatu solo run could *cost* PN seats in the north |
| **PH vulnerability** | Model assumed PH holds its battlegrounds on demographics | **Bersama siphon (3–6%)** in urban seats is a direct subtraction from PH — the Johor evidence suggests 2–4 battleground seats could flip from PH to BN/PN on this alone |
| **BN resurgence** | Johor/N9 2025–26 swings (+15pp BN) | **Confirmed and strengthened** — BN 48/56 in Johor validates the southern-resurgence scenario |

**Net read:** the updated landscape makes the government's position *stronger* than the base case suggested. The opposition's fragmentation (PAS-vs-WAWAWASAN-vs-Bersatu in Malay seats; Bersama-vs-PH in urban seats) converts what was a two-way fight into three- and four-cornered contests, which under FPTP reward the largest bloc — usually the government. The most probable GE16 outcome range shifts from "govt 137–146" toward the upper end: **141–146**, with PH's battleground losses partly offset by Bersatu/Bersama splitting the anti-government vote.

**Watch items before GE16:**
1. Whether Bersatu contests GE16 solo in its Malay heartland (would fragment the north)
2. Bersama's seat strategy — broad (spoiler) or narrow (kingmaker)
3. The Pandan and Setiawangsa by-elections — first live test of both Bersama's appeal and PKR's recovery
4. Whether PAS-allocated seat lists give WAWASAN enough winnable seats to keep it inside PN
5. The redelineation review (post-March 2026) — which will interact with all of the above
